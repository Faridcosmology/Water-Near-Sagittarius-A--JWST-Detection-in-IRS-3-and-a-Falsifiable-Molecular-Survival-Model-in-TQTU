Abstract

The NASA/ESA/CSA James Webb Space Telescope has revealed that molecular material and newly produced oxygen-rich dust can survive in the circumstellar envelope of the evolved asymptotic-giant-branch star IRS 3 in the Galactic Centre. IRS 3 lies only approximately \(0.17\) pc, or \(0.55\) light-years, from Sagittarius A* in projected separation, placing it in one of the Milky Way's most radiation-rich astrophysical environments. Webb's Mid-Infrared Instrument (MIRI) detected silicate signatures characteristic of an oxygen-rich envelope and, for the first time in this object, spectral absorption attributed to molecular water.

The observations do not require physics beyond conventional stellar-envelope chemistry: the dense, extended dusty wind of IRS 3 provides a viable shielding environment in which molecules can form or persist. However, the observation also creates an unusually stringent environment in which additional molecular-stabilization hypotheses may be tested.

This paper develops such a test within the Tanfarid Quantum Thermodynamic Universe (TQTU). Rather than interpreting the Webb discovery as evidence for TQTU, we introduce a dimensionally consistent extension of the conventional molecular-balance equation. The standard null model contains chemical production and dust-attenuated photodissociation. The TQTU extension introduces two independently testable terms: a proposed plasma–entropy recruitment contribution and a dimensionless coherence-dependent modification of molecular destruction.

The resulting model establishes a falsification criterion: if conventional radiative-transfer and chemical models reproduce the water abundance and spatial distribution of IRS 3 without requiring nonzero TQTU parameters, the additional mechanism is unnecessary. If a parameter set fixed independently predicts molecular survival across new spatial regions, epochs, or comparable Galactic-centre sources better than the conventional model, the hypothesis would merit further investigation.

1. Introduction

The Galactic Centre is an extreme astrophysical environment. Strong radiation fields, high stellar densities, shocks, stellar winds, and magnetized plasma coexist within a relatively small volume surrounding the Milky Way's central supermassive black hole, Sagittarius A*.

For this reason, the survival and production of complex dust and molecular material close to the Galactic Centre are important tests of stellar chemistry under extreme conditions.

In August 2026, an international team led by Florian Peißker reported new JWST/MIRI observations of IRS 3, an evolved star located only about

$$ 0.17\ {\rm pc} $$

from Sagittarius A* in projection, corresponding to approximately

$$ \boxed{0.55\ {\rm light\!-\!years}}. $$

The distinction in projection is important: the observed angular separation does not by itself determine the full three-dimensional distance between IRS 3 and Sagittarius A*.

The findings were published in Astronomy & Astrophysics as:

Peißker et al. (2026), “Dust production in the harsh environment of Sgr A: MIRI/JWST observation of the O-rich asymptotic giant branch star IRS 3,” A&A 712, A79.*

2. What JWST Actually Discovered

IRS 3 is an asymptotic giant branch, or AGB, star: an evolved star undergoing substantial mass loss.

JWST/MIRI obtained a continuous mid-infrared spectrum of the object and revealed strong signatures of oxygen-bearing silicate dust. Modeling of the circumstellar envelope indicates a layered dusty structure extending to roughly

$$ 10^4\ {\rm AU}, $$

with modeled dust temperatures declining from approximately \(1200\) K in inner regions toward about \(100\) K farther out.

Most strikingly, MIRI detected spectral structure attributed to

$$ \boxed{\mathrm{H_2O}} $$

within the envelope.

The water detection refers to molecular water identified spectroscopically. It does not represent liquid water, an ocean, or an imaged reservoir.

Published modeling of the shorter-wavelength water feature uses approximately

$$ T_{\mathrm{H_2O}}\sim700\ {\rm K}, $$

and

$$ N_{\mathrm{H_2O}} \sim1.5\times10^{17}\ {\rm cm^{-2}}. $$

These are spectral-model parameters rather than a measurement of the total mass of water in the entire envelope.

3. Why the Observation Matters

The discovery demonstrates that molecular chemistry and dust production remain possible remarkably close—in projected distance—to the Milky Way's central supermassive black hole.

It therefore establishes that the Galactic Centre cannot simply be treated as a chemistry-free environment.

However, it would be too strong to conclude that conventional astrophysics cannot explain the observation.

The same Webb analysis demonstrates that IRS 3 possesses an enormous, structured, optically significant circumstellar envelope produced by intense stellar mass loss. Such material can attenuate external radiation and create local conditions suitable for dust and molecular survival.

The conventional explanation therefore provides the essential scientific null hypothesis.

4. The Conventional Molecular-Survival Model

$$ \boxed{ \frac{dn_w}{dt} = \mathcal P_{\rm chem} - \Gamma_{\rm ph}\, e^{-\tau_d}\, n_w - \Gamma_{\rm other}n_w } $$

where:

$$ \mathcal P_{\rm chem} $$

$$ \Gamma_{\rm ph} $$

$$ \tau_d $$

and

$$ \Gamma_{\rm other} $$

collects other conventional destruction pathways where required.

The exponential term represents attenuation:

$$ \Gamma_{\rm eff} = \Gamma_{\rm ph}e^{-\tau_d}. $$

Thus sufficiently large optical depth can greatly suppress photodestruction without invoking additional physics.

This is the model TQTU must outperform—not bypass.

5. Steady-State Conventional Prediction

If the system is approximately stationary over the chemical timescale,

$$ \frac{dn_w}{dt}=0. $$

$$ \mathcal P_{\rm chem} = \Gamma_{\rm ph} e^{-\tau_d}n_w. $$

Hence

$$ \boxed{ n_w^{(0)} = \frac{\mathcal P_{\rm chem}} {\Gamma_{\rm ph}e^{-\tau_d}} } $$

where the superscript \(0\) denotes the conventional or null model.

If radiative transfer and stellar-envelope chemistry reproduce the observed water spectrum using this model, no additional mechanism is required.
